      Additional settings

      Detrending

      The method above is applied to every series in the undated pool. Series that are already indices are detected and carried through un-detrended — a ring width cannot be negative, and a whole file averaging 1.0 is in index units — so a .crn or a set of indices can be read straight against raw measurements. They are still put on the same scale as the detrended ones, so a mean chronology is not dominated by whichever members were detrended. Targets carry their own method, chosen per target under Data — a climate reconstruction has no growth trend to remove, and fitting a curve to one throws away the signal it was loaded for.

      Segments

      Every possible segment (a sliding window) of each series is scored against the other complete series — never against other segments — and the best few non-overlapping windows per series join the results. Segments of one series dating at inconsistent lags point to missing or false rings.

      Segment consensus

      Every run also segments each series in the background, using windows of this length: when two or more well-dated segments agree on a series' placement, that consensus lag is promoted to 1st in the results — badged, with the evidence in the tooltip. Chronology runs read the segments against the mean chronology; pairwise runs read them against the target series, and only against it — a consensus about a series nobody is dating against says nothing, and every pair would be a grid per pair. With the Segments tool above enabled, consensus reads that run's segments instead (its own length setting).

      Plots

      Composite target

      Averaging targets only helps if they agree. Two that share no signal do not reinforce each other — the mean is mostly the noise they do not have in common — and two that agree better a few years apart are two of which one is dated wrong, which a composite hides behind a tidy-looking mean. Every ticked pair is measured over the years they share, as dated and at every lag within ±10 years, before the mean is used for anything. The members are drawn together below with their mean through them — scroll to zoom, drag to pan, double-click to reset.

      Missing / false ring test

      Simulates every possible single-ring correction of one series: each measured ring is split in two (as if a ring boundary was missed inside it) and each neighbouring pair is merged (as if one ring was falsely counted as two). Every edit is re-detrended and fully re-crossdated against the reference — edits that beat the unedited baseline bear fruit. Fruitful corrections can be applied and the test re-run iteratively (each pass hunts the next missing / false ring), and any corrected series can be downloaded as .rwl. You can also test one ring of your own choosing — a ring you suspect under the scope, or one ranked below the table — without running the full sweep.

      Build chronology

      Grow a chronology iteratively — crossdate one candidate series at a time against the current mean chronology, review the alignment plots, then approve the suggested lag (or your own override). Uses the current detrend settings and, if a chronology was loaded, seeds from its members.

      Measuring needs a Chromium browser over https

      This browser does not expose the Web Serial API, so it cannot talk to a measuring stage. Use Chrome, Edge or Opera (Firefox 151+ also works), and open this page over https or http://localhost — on a file:// page the serial API is unavailable no matter the browser. Everything else in RingdateR works here.

      Measure a core

      Record ring widths from a Velmex VRO stage straight into the undated pool, then crossdate without leaving the page. Nothing is uploaded; the serial port is opened locally by your browser. Settings are fixed at the VRO's 9600 8N1, CR-terminated protocol. Every ring is auto-saved in this browser as you measure, so a closed tab, a refresh or a flat battery does not cost you the core — the session comes back when you return.
